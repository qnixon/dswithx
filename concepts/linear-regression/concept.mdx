Linear regression finds the straight line $\hat{y} = a + bx$ that best fits a
set of points, where "best" means the line that minimizes the sum of squared
vertical distances from the points to the line (least squares).

## The dataset

`data.csv` holds 100 synthetic students: weekly study `hours` and exam
`score`. It was generated once by `make_data.py` (a line with slope 5.2 and
intercept 42, plus normal noise; fixed seed 20261030) and committed.

## The math

With means $\bar{x}$ and $\bar{y}$, define the sums of squares and
cross-products

$$
S_{xx} = \sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \qquad
S_{yy} = \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2 \qquad
S_{xy} = \sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})
$$

Then the least-squares slope and intercept, and the correlation $r$, are

$$
b = \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}} \qquad a = \bar{y} - b\,\bar{x} \qquad
r = \frac{S_{xy}}{\sqrt{S_{xx}\,S_{yy}}}
$$

$r^2$ is the share of the variation in scores that the line explains.
