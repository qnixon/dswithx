k-means groups points into $k$ clusters. Each cluster has a centroid, and
the algorithm alternates between assigning points to their nearest centroid
and moving each centroid to the mean of its points, until nothing changes.

## The dataset

`data.csv` holds 150 synthetic points, `x` and `y`, drawn from three
overlapping blobs. It was generated once by `make_data.py` (fixed seed
20261101) and committed. The first three rows come from different blobs.

## The method

With $k = 3$:

1. Start with the first three points as the centroids.
2. **Assign** every point to its nearest centroid by Euclidean distance.
3. If no point changed cluster since the last assignment, stop.
4. **Update** each centroid to the mean of its assigned points, then go back
   to step 2.

`iterations` counts the assignment steps, including the final one where
nothing changed. `inertia` is the sum of squared distances from each point to
its centroid:

$$
\text{inertia} = \sum_{i} \lVert p_i - c_{a(i)} \rVert^2
$$

The generator made sure no point is ever (almost) equally close to two
centroids, so every implementation follows the same path.
