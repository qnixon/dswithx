k-nearest neighbors (kNN) classifies a new point by looking at the $k$
labelled points closest to it and taking a majority vote. There is no
training step: the "model" is the training data itself.

## The dataset

`data.csv` holds 200 synthetic wheat seeds of two varieties, `kama` and
`rosa`, with kernel `length_mm` and `width_mm`. The `split` column marks 150
rows as `train` and 50 as `test`. It was generated once by `make_data.py`
(fixed seed 20261031) and committed.

## The method

For each test seed, with $k = 5$:

1. Compute the Euclidean distance to every training seed:
   $d = \sqrt{(\ell_1 - \ell_2)^2 + (w_1 - w_2)^2}$.
2. Take the 5 nearest training seeds.
3. Predict the variety most of them have. With two varieties and an odd $k$,
   the vote cannot tie.

The result is the share of test seeds predicted correctly (accuracy). The
generator made sure no test seed has a near-tie for 5th-nearest neighbor, so
every implementation finds the same neighbors.
